INSTITUTIONAL ASSURANCE

Institutional Reasoning Assessment

A preliminary, surface-level assessment of how an institution currently reasons, decides, commits, persists, learns and governs across consequential activity.

Important: This is a preliminary assessment only. It does not produce a diagnostic score, certification or institutional finding. Its purpose is to identify areas where a deeper, evidence-based assessment may be warranted.

A starting point, not a verdict.

Please answer from the institution's current practice rather than from its intended policy. The questions are deliberately concise. Where an answer raises a material question, a deeper assessment can examine the underlying evidence, decision history and operational state.

At the end, you will be asked whether you would be willing to provide evidence if a deeper assessment were undertaken.

1. Institutional Profile

Basic context for interpreting the preliminary responses.

2. REASON

How the institution develops and challenges interpretations of evidence.

When evidence is ambiguous or competing interpretations exist, are alternatives explicitly challenged?

Can a consequential recommendation be traced to the reasoning that produced it?

Are significant assumptions and counter-arguments preserved when a conclusion is reached?

3. DISTINGUISH

How evidence, interpretation, judgement, decision and outcome are kept distinct.

Can decision-makers distinguish recorded evidence from interpretation or opinion?

Are material changes in interpretation visible rather than silently becoming “facts”?

Can the institution reconstruct what was known, believed and decided at the time?

4. DECIDE

How legitimate institutional conclusions are established.

Is authority to make consequential decisions explicitly defined?

Can the institution distinguish an analytical recommendation from an authorized decision?

Is accountability for the final decision identifiable after the event?

5. COMMIT

How authorized decisions become controlled operational action.

Is there a defined transition from an authorized decision to operational execution?

Can operational teams identify what they are authorized to change and what they are not?

Are consequential deviations from an authorized decision recorded and governed?

6. PERSIST

What consequential institutional state survives across time, people and systems.

When key people leave, does consequential reasoning remain understandable and usable?

Are commitments and unresolved questions preserved separately from ordinary document storage?

Can the institution reconstruct why an important position changed?

7. LEARN

How experience changes future institutional behaviour without uncontrolled adaptation.

Are consequential outcomes reviewed against the reasoning and decision that produced them?

Does learning produce explicit institutional changes rather than informal lessons only?

Can the institution identify what changed because of an observed outcome?

8. GOVERN

Who controls changes in institutional knowledge, commitments and operating state.

Is there explicit authority over changes to consequential institutional state?

Can computational or AI systems change institutional state without human authorization?

Are material state changes attributable to an accountable human authority?

9. Consequential Decision & Evidence Path

This does not request the underlying evidence. It identifies whether a deeper examination could be grounded in a real institutional event.
